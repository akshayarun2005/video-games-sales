01 · Introduction

Making sense of a market with thousands of titles

The global video game industry spans many genres, platforms and regional audiences. Raw sales tables make it hard to see which categories perform strongly and how preferences differ.

This project performs exploratory data analysis (EDA) on the VGChartz video game sales dataset: 16,598 games with 11 attributes (rank, name, platform, release year, genre, publisher, and North America, Europe, Japan, other and global sales).

Python (Pandas, NumPy) is used for cleaning and aggregation, and Matplotlib and Seaborn for visualization. Sales are compared by genre, platform, publisher, region and release year, first for the 100 best-selling titles and then for the full dataset. The findings are published as this interactive web dashboard.

03 · Research gaps

What the literature leaves open

  1. 1

    Most surveyed studies focus on predicting sales with regression or machine learning, and give limited descriptive, visual comparison of sales by genre, platform, publisher and region.

  2. 2

    Several studies rely on small or restricted samples (for example 351 PC games, or European sales only), so their findings may not generalize across platforms and regions.

  3. 3

    Conceptual work on the industry provides frameworks, but little quantitative evidence from sales data.

  4. 4

    Few studies compare conclusions drawn from best-selling titles with those drawn from the whole market.

  5. 5

    Analyses are rarely delivered as reusable, interactive tools that non-technical users can explore.

04 · Problem statement

Raw sales tables hide the story

The video game market includes many genres, platforms and regional audiences. Raw sales tables make it difficult to identify which categories and platforms perform strongly, how regional preferences differ, and how the sales of leading titles vary across release years.

Problem

To design a reusable exploratory analysis and visualization workflow, and an interactive dashboard, that compares global and regional video game sales by genre, platform, publisher and release year using the VGChartz dataset (16,598 games), and shows how far results from the top-100 titles hold for the full dataset.

Scope: descriptive analysis only; no machine-learning prediction is required.

    12 · Limitations & data notes

    What these results can and can't tell you

    Sample vs the whole market

    The project analysis covers the 100 best-selling titles, so it describes blockbusters. Switch the dashboard to "All games" to see how the conclusions change across every record.

    Units, not revenue

    Sales are in millions of units sold. Price differences between platforms and eras mean units are not the same as money earned.

    Estimates and gaps

    VGChartz figures are estimates, not audited publisher data. Coverage thins after 2016 (344 titles in 2016, 3 in 2017). 271 games have no release year and 58 have no publisher, so they are left out of those charts.

    Descriptive, not causal

    The analysis shows what happened, not why. For example, lower totals in recent years may reflect incomplete data or newer titles having had less time to sell.

    About the numbers on this page: every figure is computed directly from the project's CSV (vgsales.csv, 16,598 records) with pandas, and the top-100 results match the charts in the original presentation. The original Matplotlib charts are one click away under each top-100 chart.

    • The project demonstrates a complete beginner-level data analysis workflow.

    • The output includes a cleaned CSV file and reusable charts.
    • Results are descriptive and limited to the included top-100 sample.
    • Comparing the sample with the full dataset shows how much blockbuster titles shape the picture.
